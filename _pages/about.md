I received my Ph.D. in Informatics from the Graduate Institute for Advanced Studies, SOKENDAI (National Institute of Informatics, Tokyo, Japan) in September 2026, advised by Prof. Kensuke Fukuda. Before that, I received my bachelor's and master's degrees from the University of Tokyo.

My doctoral research characterized global IPv6 scanning activities via proactive network telescopes. I designed and operated an IPv6-native hybrid telescope — a passive darknet paired with a responsive honeynet — continuously for nearly three years, and used controlled exposure (DNS, NTP Pool) to study how Internet scanners discover fresh IPv6 prefixes and how their behavior differs across exposure mechanisms and protocol versions (IPv4 vs. IPv6).
Before that, I also worked on Age of Information in IoT systems, Named Data Networking, video streaming and data caching.

My research interests include:
- Network threat defense: deception technology (honeypots, darknets), proactive threat hunting
- Network measurement: IPv6/IPv4 scanning, Internet-scale traffic analysis
- High-performance networking: programmable data planes (P4), RDMA, eBPF, congestion control
- Systems for AI: in-network gradient aggregation, KV-cache transport for LLM inference
